What Actually Goes Into an Accurate Estimate

A real kitchen remodel estimate isn't a single number pulled from a quick look around your kitchen — it's built from several specific inputs:

  • Square footage and current layout — a straightforward reface costs very differently than moving plumbing and electrical for a new layout.
  • Scope of work — are you replacing cabinets, countertops, and flooring, or doing a lighter refresh of just one or two elements?
  • Material selections — cabinet grade, countertop material, and flooring type all swing the total significantly.
  • Structural changes — removing a wall, adding an island, or relocating a sink adds cost beyond materials and finish work.
  • Labor and permitting — costs vary by region, and any layout change typically requires a permit.

An estimate that skips most of these and gives you a single flat number after a five-minute conversation is a guess, not an estimate.

What to Prepare Before You Request an Estimate

The more specific information you bring to the first conversation, the more accurate your estimate will be. Before you call anyone, put together:

  • Rough measurements of your kitchen, even if they're not exact.
  • Photos of your current kitchen from a few different angles, including cabinets, countertops, and any problem areas.
  • A budget range — even a wide one helps a contractor recommend materials that fit rather than wasting time on options outside your range.
  • Inspiration photos or a Pinterest board showing styles, colors, and layouts you like.
  • A rough timeline — are you hoping to start in the next month, or planning for next year?

Questions That Separate a Real Quote From a Guess

When you're comparing kitchen remodel estimates, ask each contractor:

  1. Is this quote itemized, or a single lump sum?
  2. What specific materials and brands are included in this price?
  3. Does this include demo and disposal of my existing kitchen?
  4. What's not included, and what would trigger a change order?
  5. What's the realistic timeline from signing to completion?

A contractor who can answer all five clearly and in writing is giving you a real estimate. Vague answers to more than one or two of these are a warning sign.

Why Written, Itemized Quotes Matter

Two estimates for the "same" kitchen can differ by thousands of dollars simply because one assumes builder-grade materials and the other assumes mid-range finishes — without ever stating that difference out loud. An itemized quote forces that comparison into the open: you can see exactly what cabinet line, countertop material, and labor scope each number is based on, rather than comparing two mystery totals.

The lowest number on paper isn't always the best deal — it's only a good deal if it includes the same scope and materials as the higher quotes you're comparing it to.

Estimates vary based on assumed materials, whether layout changes are included, regional labor rates, and how thoroughly the full scope is accounted for. A vague verbal number and a detailed written quote can differ by thousands for the same kitchen.
